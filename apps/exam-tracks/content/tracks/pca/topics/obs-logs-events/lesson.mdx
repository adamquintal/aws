## Three kinds of telemetry

Systems emit **telemetry**, data about their behaviour, as three main **signals**: **metrics**, **logs** and **traces**. This topic compares the first two. Traces are next.

## What a log is

The OpenTelemetry primer defines a **log** as a **timestamped message emitted by services or other components**. Unlike traces, logs aren't necessarily tied to a particular request. A sample (from the primer):

```text
I, [2021-02-23T13:26:23.505892 #22473]  INFO -- : [6459ffe1-ea53-4044-aaa3-bf902868f730] Started GET "/" for ::1 at 2021-02-23 13:26:23 -0800
```

- **Structured logs** have a consistent schema (typed fields, often JSON). They're preferred in production because they're easy to parse, validate and correlate.
- **Unstructured logs** are free text: readable, but hard to analyse at scale.

### And events?

An **event** is a record that something specific happened: a login, a deploy, a failed payment. In OpenTelemetry's model, **events are a special type of log**: all events are logs, but not all logs are events. A log record carries a timestamp, a body and attributes, and an event adds an event name.

## Metrics vs logs

| | Metrics | Logs / events |
|---|---|---|
| What | Numbers **aggregated over time** (error rate, CPU, request rate) | **Individual** timestamped records with detail |
| Cost | Small and fixed per series | Grows with every event |
| Best for | Dashboards, trends, **alerting** | Investigating **one specific** incident |
| Typical question | "Is the error rate rising?" | "What happened to order 4711?" |

## Where Prometheus fits

Prometheus is firmly on the **metrics** side. The FAQ's answer to "How do I feed logs into Prometheus?" is **"Don't!"**. Use a log system such as **Grafana Loki** or **OpenSearch**. Prometheus collects and processes metrics; it isn't an event logging system.

## Making them work together

- **Count what you log.** The instrumentation guide says every logging line should have a **counter** you increment, so you can see how often a message happens. Also export totals of info, warning and error lines, and compare them across releases.
- **Keep the detail in logs.** IDs, messages and payloads belong in the log record, never in metric labels (that's unbounded cardinality).
- **Correlate.** Logs are much more useful when they carry the **trace and span IDs** of the request they belong to, which leads straight into tracing.
