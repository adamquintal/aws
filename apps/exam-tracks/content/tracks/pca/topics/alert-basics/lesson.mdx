## The philosophy in one line

The alerting guide sums it up: **keep alerting simple, alert on symptoms, have good dashboards to pinpoint causes, and avoid pages where there is nothing to do.**

The Zen of Prometheus adds: alerts should be **urgent, important, actionable and real**, and **don't over-alert, because alert fatigue is real**.

## Symptoms page, causes don't

| | Symptom-based | Cause-based |
|---|---|---|
| Example | "Checkout latency p99 > 2s", "5% of requests failing" | "Database CPU at 95%", "one replica down" |
| Users feel it? | Yes | Not necessarily |
| Where it goes | **A page**, waking someone | Dashboards and ticket queues, handled at leisure |

You still collect cause metrics, and they're how you debug, but they shouldn't wake anyone unless users are affected.

## What to alert on, by service type

- **Online serving:** alert on **latency and error rates, as high up the stack as possible**. Page on latency at **one point** only. If a lower component is slow but users are fine, don't page. Page on **user-visible errors**. Add separate pages only for invisible failures that are serious, such as losing money.
- **Offline processing:** the key metric is **how long data takes to get through**. Page when that delay hurts users.
- **Batch jobs:** page if the job **hasn't succeeded recently enough** to cause problems. Allow **at least two full runs**: a job every 4 hours taking 1 hour gets a threshold of about **10 hours**. If you can't tolerate one failed run, run it more often.
- **Capacity:** nearing capacity isn't user pain yet, but often needs a human soon, so it's worth an alert.

## Tolerate blips

Allow slack for small blips. Prometheus alerting rules have a **`for` duration**: the condition must stay true that long before the alert fires. Without it, one failed scrape could page someone. The Zen's rule of thumb is **at least 5 minutes** unless you have a reason otherwise. The next topic covers the syntax.

## Watch the watchers

**Metamonitoring** means alerting when Prometheus, Alertmanager, the Pushgateway and the rest of the monitoring stack aren't working. As always, prefer symptoms: an end-to-end check that an alert really travels from the Pushgateway to Prometheus to Alertmanager to your inbox beats separate alerts on each piece. External **blackbox** monitoring also catches problems your internal monitoring can't see.

At the very least, alert on whether your targets are present and healthy. Prometheus generates `up` for every target for exactly this:

```promql
up == 0
```

## Naming

Alert names can contain any characters, but the community convention is **CamelCase**: `InstanceDown`, `HighRequestLatency`.
